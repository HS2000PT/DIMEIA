\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:introduction}

This chapter introduces the dissertation. It motivates the work, states the problem, defines the objectives and
research questions, summarises the scientific contributions, and outlines the structure of the document.

\section{Motivation and Overview}
\label{sec:intro_motivation}

The United States equity market is the largest in the world and is owned, directly or indirectly, by a majority
of US households: 62\% of Americans reported owning stock in 2025 \autocite{gallup2025stock}, in a market whose
capitalisation reached US\$62.2~trillion at the end of 2024 \autocite{sifma2025factbook}. At the same time,
artificial intelligence has been adopted rapidly across financial services \autocite{ccaf2026aifs}, yet many of
the resulting models behave as opaque ``black boxes'' whose reasoning users cannot inspect
\autocite{arrieta2020xai}. Chapter~\ref{chap:contextualization} develops this context in detail.

For the non-professional (``retail'') investor, this combination is difficult. Markets move continuously across
thousands of tickers and financial news arrives without pause, but the tools that help to interpret these
signals are typically institutional, opaque, or both. This dissertation addresses that gap with an
\emph{explainable} financial-alert system: one that not only flags relevant events but also explains them in a
way the investor can follow, verify and act upon.

\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:intro_problem}

A retail investor who wishes to stay informed faces two distinct problems. First, distinguishing a genuinely
abnormal price movement from ordinary volatility requires statistical judgement and constant attention. Second,
when a relevant news item appears, assessing its likely impact requires knowledge of how analogous past events
affected prices --- information that is rarely at hand. Existing consumer tools tend to deliver alerts without
explanation, or rely on models whose internal logic is hidden, which is precisely the transparency problem that
explainable AI seeks to address \autocite{arrieta2020xai, adadi2018peeking}.

The problem this work tackles is therefore not price prediction, but \emph{explanation}: detecting abrupt market
movements and relevant news, and presenting, for each alert, a traceable account of what was detected, the
reasoning applied, the sources, and concrete historical precedents of similar events and their observed impact.

\section{Objectives and Research Questions}
\label{sec:intro_objectives}

The general objective is to design, implement and evaluate an explainable, reproducible financial-alert system
for US retail investors, driven by two independent triggers (abrupt market movements and incoming news) and
delivered through Telegram. This is pursued through three research questions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RQ1.} Can transparent statistical methods detect abrupt market movements with enough precision
        to be useful to a retail investor, while remaining explainable?
  \item \textbf{RQ2.} Can a news--market correlation engine retrieve genuinely analogous historical news and
        quantify their observed price impact, without lookahead, better than trivial baselines?
  \item \textbf{RQ3.} Can the resulting explanations be made faithful to the system's actual logic and useful to
        a retail investor?
\end{itemize}

The supporting objectives are: (i) build a thin end-to-end slice early; (ii) develop each component in its
simplest defensible form; (iii) evaluate each component against a design defined beforehand
(Chapter~\ref{chap:evaluation}); and (iv) keep the whole system reproducible and transparent.

\section{Scientific Contributions}
\label{sec:intro_contributions}

This is an Artificial Intelligence \emph{Engineering} dissertation. The contribution is not to invent new
algorithms but to \emph{integrate, apply and critically evaluate} existing components in a functional,
explainable and reproducible system. Concretely, the contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item A documented, reproducible methodology for \textbf{news--market impact correlation}, combining sentence
        embeddings for retrieval with event-study windows for impact measurement, with explicit choices of
        similarity metric and window and explicit avoidance of lookahead.
  \item An \textbf{XAI-first alerting pipeline} that exposes its full reasoning chain (detection, evidence,
        sources, precedents) to a non-expert user.
  \item A \textbf{critical evaluation} of each component against baselines and a transparent design, reporting
        honest results and limitations.
\end{itemize}
